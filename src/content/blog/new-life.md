## 2021 - Linguistics, NLP, and the pre-ChatGPT "AGI" dream

> Making computers understand human language is the first step towards building truly intelligent machines. The more computers understand us, the more they can mimic our qualities such as creativity, and the more we can invent or discover with their help.

This was an excerpt from my university application essay, translated from Japanese. 

I was already a huge linguistics nerd. I even went on to conquer our national linguistics olympiad, though did not pursue it further. One of the concepts in computational linguistics (though after graduating with a CS degree, a better term would be natural language processing) that fascinated me was the idea of "semantic arithmetic". 

Because we can represent words as vectors, we can perform arithmetic on them. The most notable example when you look up about this is the famous `king - man + woman = queen` example. This idea still influences the way I reason and use my coding agents to this day. I would go:

> See this logic in `king.rs`'s code? Try to implement the same logic for `queen.rs`!

All of this was very exciting to me, especially at the time when GPT-3 was deep in private beta. I dreamed of a future where I could build my own research lab that gradually builds up proofs, discoveries, and inventions in the field of computer science and mathematics. I did not picture the world would have changed this much during my time in university, and I was very excited to be a part of it.

> But where is your personal lab?

## 2021 - Productivity freak VS Exam that made you question your system

I LOVED [Thomas Frank](https://www.youtube.com/@ThomasFrank) and [Ali Abdaal](https://www.youtube.com/@AliAbdaal). I was obsessed with ricing my Notion, filling my calendar with time blocks, and optimizing my workflow. We would call this a "productivity freak" phase. I rigorously tracked how I spent my time. I always kept a journal to reflect on my day. I used spaced repetition to memorize all the Japanese vocabularies and kanjis. My Anki deck was filled with cards. You know, the usual productivity stuff.

But then my first post-highschool, pre-university (let's say it's a placement test for my university) exam came, consisting of Math, Chemistry, and Physics. I was very confident in my answers. It was relatively easy for me. Pfft! I did academic olympiads in both Math and Physics, and I was a chemistry nerd. I literally sang the periodic table song in my childhood. 

Then I absolutely bombed the exam. 

Yeah you probably guessed from how I built the narrative. But I took this loss very personally. I concluded that this was a complete failure, and productivity is a propaganda by Notion to sell more templates. (joke) I decided that I would never make a system again, always trust my instincts, and just wing it.

The second exam came, and I did not study at all. Well, I did, but at most a day or two before the exam. I held on to [Parkinson's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson%27s_law) and [Pareto Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle) religiously. 

> I will just do the 20% of the work, hell, even 10% of the time, and get 80% of the results!

And it worked! I got 90% in every subject. That single strategy alone landed me a spot in one of the top universities.

I was... at least not disappointed? My logic was that at least I could say to others that my system was not a waste of time, and at the same time, I could say to myself that I could achieve good results without it. Looking back, I think this was a very toxic mindset. I was basically a performative overachiever. I cared more about how I looked to others to the point where I lied to myself about how much effort I put in. It's like you are benchmarking an algorithm but you put the start time at the very beginning of the core part of the work, and end time at the very end of it, but you ignore the time it took to preprocess or export the results. I don't know if this is a good analogy.

What about the university I got into? People say that getting into a top university will pressure you to perform, and keep your head in the game. But I thought it was the opposite for me. I felt like getting into a lower-tier university would push me to work harder and always prove myself that "hey, I don't need to be in a top university to achieve great things". That way, I can highlight other aspects of myself in my resume to compensate for the lack of a prestigious university name. But getting into a top university felt like a golden ticket that I could just coast on. I thought, "people would be willing to look at other parts of my resume instead of **having** to look at other parts of my resume".

With that ill logic aside, I carried on the dream of building personal AGI research lab into the university. I was very excited to take a lot of liberal arts courses, and learn about a lot of different subjects. I thought this would help me build a more well-rounded understanding of the world, help me empathize with different perspectives, and build innovative solutions to problems.

## 2022 - I don't wanna play badminton anymore. 

For context, I have been playing badminton since I was in elementary school. I was pretty good at it, and I enjoyed it a lot. I liked the skill ceiling of how you can be very strategic with your shot placement, and how you can outplay your opponent with agility and swift movements. 

Our badminton club is divided into days for different skill levels: "beginner" and "advanced"; no places for the in-betweens. I learned that it was not fun at all to play with people who are much weaker than you. I felt like I tried so hard to place that shot in the corner, but had I just winged it and placed it in an approximately correct place, they would not have tried to run for it anyway. I felt like playing with people who are better than you gives you a lot of excitement and motivation to improve. They appreciate your good shots, and you can learn a lot from them. Until I got humbled hard.

The people from the "advanced" day were damn good. They were relentless with their smashes, and they had very good physical condition. I was not used to that level of intensity, and I got tired very quickly. I felt like learning from people much better than you is a myth. So, the experience was completely binary.
