# Nothing

There is nothing left to do so there are more things to do.

---

You cut down a tree[^1], it drops a sapling, you plant the sapling.
Now you have unlimited woods.

[^1]: I think it's a good assumption that my readers play Minecraft. If you don't, then you can just imagine that you cut down a tree in real life and it drops a sapling, and you plant the sapling. Now you have unlimited wood.

---

You trap villagers in a small area, and you breed them[^2]. Threaten them with zombies, and they will spawn iron golems to protect themselves. Now you have unlimited iron.

[^2]: If you're still imagining doing this in real life, please stop beyond this point.

---

You can then trade the sticks for emeralds, and the emeralds for diamond armor and tools.

Now your entire playthrough is solved.

---

You never have to mine. You never have to explore. You never have to fight. You never have to do anything.

---

You close the game, and go back to your life.

---

## Is it anything different?

---

In 2026, journalism is a dying art. Copy-writing doesn't need to be done by humans anymore.

Illustrations? That can be fully generated. Audio? Music? Video? Nothing survives the revolution.

---

Even interior design, product design, and architecture are being replaced. Decision making is also trivial.

The world is being run by complex algorithms that pattern-match and optimize every aspect of our lives.

---

So you say "because software developers made it, they must be the only jobs that are safe?"

---

Nope, you can one-shot a state-of-the-art full-stack software that can maintain and deploy itself, plan and implement new features, and even write its own documentation.

Testing? QA? Code reviews? All done by the software itself.

---

Real life isn't any different.
