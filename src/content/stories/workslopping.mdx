## The 5th Industrial Revolution

I can't deny that LLM has improved my productivity tremendously.

Not only does it help me write code, but I would have never put effort into

- Cross-referencing between different sources
- Running ablation tests to trim down bloats
- Writing a newsletter (jk lol)

---

And, I'm sure it's not just me.

That's why I have never been strongly against it.
Please do use it to your advantage.

I'm happy to see people with non-technical backgrounds leveraging LLM to create new software products.

Non-artistic people are now able to create _art_ and design for their technical work to be more presentable.

---

So... what went wrong?

How come we still associate LLM with low-quality slop?

But being able to say "I don't know" is a good start.

---

People are evidently[^1] afraid of leaving holes in their work.

It's embarassing to admit "Oh, that was an oversight on my part. I haven't gotten around to that yet." So people tend to go with:

> I'd rather blindly trust AI's advice than admit that I don't know.

[^1]: AI advice suppresses people's willingness to say "I don't know", even when the advice is wrong and accuracy is incentivized (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13562)

---

This happens a lot with people in high-context society where people tend to favor hinting you to get the answer yourself instead of asking directly.

This makes admitting "I don't know" even harder, because you are afraid to be told

> That's your job to figure out.

> Just Google it. Ask ChatGPT.

---

Humbling, but hear me out. If someone actually tells you that, it says more about them than you.

The fact that you are here to ask shows that you

---

AI doesn't make people lazy.

It makes being lazy more _actionable_.

You used to be able to get lazy students go about their day without doing their homework.

Now suddenly they learned to fuсk around more yet never find out.

---

AI doesn't produce low-quality work

In fact, it's even worse. It produces high-quality work that is _indistinguishable_ to decide whether human judgment was applied or not.

---

Copy-and-paste an entire Wikipedia article?

Google Translate your homework from your native language in an English class?

Take images from copyrighted sources and slap them on your work without attribution?

### These have been around for decades.

---

We had a hard time explaining it because saying

> It strips away the human judgment and defeats the purpose of learning and evaluation

<Image src={EmojiNerd} alt="🤔" class="w-3/5 max-w-2xl" />

...is nerd as _sнit_.

---

In other words, it takes a high level of self-awareness to examine yourself through a systemic lens.

Don't get me wrong. This has nothing to do with intelligence or quality as a person. In fact, I would argue it's what makes a human a human.

Not often do you stop and think about how your actions affect the world around you, nor how the world around you affects your actions.

...But that's a story for another time.

---

So what we did was we relied on the fact that it was still "low-quality" and well-distinguishable from work done by effort and judgment.

Our teachers were able to say "Hey, don't copy from Wikipedia. Wikipedia is not a reliable source." just because

### It was actually unreliable at the time

---

Now it's not. Wikipedia is actually relatively reliable with the effort of contributors working day and night to make it so.

Saying "you should double-check your sources" makes it sounds like "because it might be wrong" so this argument starts to lose its weight.

---

It might still be wrong, but more importantly, you never stop to think:

> But what if there is a better explanation?
>
> Perhaps I could find another source that discusses the counterargument of this
>
> Why there were so many edits on this article

---

So this argument of "AI-slops" will only hold once it stops becoming "slops".

One might say you should be grateful that it still produces low enough quality to be distinguishable from human effort.

This article is handwritten by me. AI probably writes faster, better, and more coherently. Why would I still handwrite it?

---

> Once it doesn't matter anymore who did the work, then it starts to matter more who did the work.

Sorry if it sounds like a shift-word-around "Don't love your job; job your love"-ass quote.

The point is that "who did the work" credibility will soon be replaced by "who did the work" accountability.

---

## I don't care

---

Caring, acting, and taking responsibility are three different things.

And AI lowering the barrier to entry for all three of them is a breach of trust in the system.

---

Two failure modes here:

1. It makes it easier to pretend you care and not take responsibility for your actions. Once you pull the "**Claude said:**" card, you distance yourself from the words and actions you should have owned.

2. People who genuinely care are now forced to train themselves to be more skeptical. "Ah yes, this is wrong, but didn't you see it has an AI label to it? **You should have doubted**" and this is now their fault for not noticing their coworker's mistake.

---

### Stamps of approval are being used sparingly

> Everybody will have to verify their own work now

is a good rule to have for sure, but it is only a burden to those who trust this contract.

A _"malicious"_ actor would just hand you over their part of the work, only for you to realize the rule is there to trick your assumption that they are trustworthy.

---

Ironically, the rule becomes an unspoken rule once it is explicitly spoken.

You now stopped asking "did you even verify your work?" because it's apparently now offensive to be skeptical that they did not follow the agreed-upon rule.

Because you know they would just say "well I did verify my work," there is no point in asking.

---

Coming back to "malicious" actors that I have put in quotes, are they really malicious?

Let me say this once more: _I don't know._

---

But I like to think that being intentionally malicious at work is not an effective strategy at all.

Most employees on the same level as you do not have bad intentions. They have more things to lose than to gain. They are just trying to get by and do their work.

This just naturally happens when you have a system that punishes people for being efficient and loving their work.
